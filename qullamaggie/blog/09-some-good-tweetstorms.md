## Recurring Themes in the Tweetstorms

### 1. Focus on High Relative Strength Stocks
Trade stocks showing **strong relative strength** compared to the broader market — specifically those forming fresh breakouts on high volume. If the overall market pulls back 3% and your stock barely moves, that relative strength is a powerful signal.

### 2. The Importance of Cutting Losses Quickly
One of the most repeated themes. **Cut losers fast, let winners run.** Small losses are the tuition for learning to trade; large losses are the avoidable mistakes of traders who hold hope over process.

### 3. Multi-Month Breakouts
The best trades come from stocks breaking out of **multi-week or multi-month consolidation patterns**. A clear base with a tight range and declining volume into the breakout (followed by expanding volume on the break) is the gold standard setup.

### 4. Scaling Out of Winning Positions
Kullamägi describes a **tiered exit strategy**:
- Sell a portion at the first meaningful target (often after a 10–20% move).
- Move the stop to break-even on the remainder.
- Let the core position run as far as the trend allows.
- Only exit fully when price decisively breaks the key moving average (often the 10- or 20-day SMA).

### 5. Be in Cash During Choppy Markets
During unclear or choppy market conditions, **the correct position is often cash**. There is no rule that says you must be in a trade at all times. Preserving capital and mental clarity during poor conditions is as important as being aggressive during optimal ones.

### 6. Pre-Market Preparation Is Non-Negotiable
Every successful trading day starts with:
- A curated **watchlist** of the strongest stocks.
- **Price alerts** set at key breakout levels.
- A clear **game plan**: entry trigger, stop level, share count.
- No surprises — if the stock triggers your level, you already know exactly what to do.

### 7. Trader Psychology
Key psychological lessons from the tweetstorms:
- **Revenge trading** (trying to immediately recover a loss) is one of the most destructive behaviours.
- **Overtrading** — forcing trades when setups are not present — erodes capital and confidence simultaneously.
- **Objectivity** requires separating yourself from your open positions; your portfolio's P&L should not drive your market opinions.

### 8. Learning from Mistakes Through Trade Reviews
Kullamägi regularly posts **trade post-mortems** — including losses — in his tweetstorms. The discipline of honest self-review, identifying exactly where you deviated from your process, is what separates improving traders from those who stagnate.

### 9. Market Leaders Do Not Stay Leaders Forever
Even the strongest momentum stocks eventually exhaust their moves. Learning to recognise **distribution patterns** (high volume on down days, failed breakout attempts, lower highs) is critical for exiting before the trend reverses.

### 10. Size Into Your Best Ideas
When a setup is genuinely A+ quality — the right stock, right market conditions, right technical setup — **size up appropriately**. Many traders who understand setups intellectually still fail because they trade everything at the same conservative size regardless of quality.

## Quoted Trading Principles

> *"Be in the best stocks in the best sectors. Everything else is noise."*

> *"Most traders lose money because they cut their winners and let their losers run. The successful ones do the opposite."*

> *"The market doesn't care about your P&L or your feelings. It will only reward disciplined, rules-based decision-making."*

> *"Preparation is everything. If you don't have a plan before the open, you're improvising — and improvising in trading usually means losing."*
