# NASDAQ Comparison: Late 1990s vs. Today

**Source:** [qullamaggie.com/nasdaq-comparison-late-90s-vs-today/](https://qullamaggie.com/nasdaq-comparison-late-90s-vs-today/)  
**Author:** Kristjan Kullamägi (Qullamaggie)  
**Published:** July 2020

---

## Overview

Written in the aftermath of the COVID-19 market crash and subsequent V-shaped recovery, this post compares the NASDAQ's behaviour in mid-2020 to two prior historic analogues: the recovery in **late 1998** (after the LTCM/Russia crisis correction) and the broader **late 1990s bull run** that ultimately ended in the dot-com bubble burst of 2000.

---

## The Observation: V-Shaped Recoveries

Kullamägi noted that after major corrections, the NASDAQ had twice produced **massive V-shaped rallies** of 40%+ in just 3 months, rebounding hard off the 10- and 20-day simple moving averages (SMAs):

1. **1998 Recovery:** After the Russian debt default and LTCM collapse caused a sharp correction, the NASDAQ staged a violent V-shaped recovery, going almost straight up for months before eventually topping out in the parabolic 1999–2000 frenzy.
2. **2020 Recovery:** The COVID crash was the sharpest bear market in history (fell ~35% in ~5 weeks), followed by an equally rapid recovery as stimulus and tech demand drove the NASDAQ back to all-time highs.

---

## Key Observations

| Factor | 1998 | 2020 |
|--------|------|------|
| Correction trigger | Russian default / LTCM | COVID-19 pandemic |
| Recovery speed | Very fast (weeks to months) | Very fast (weeks to months) |
| Character | Dip buyers rewarded; shallow pullbacks | Dip buyers rewarded; shallow pullbacks |
| Moving average behaviour | 10/20 SMA acted as support throughout | 10/20 SMA acted as support throughout |
| Result (near-term) | NASDAQ continued higher for 1.5+ years | Strong bull run through 2020–2021 |

---

## The Investor Psychology Trap

Kullamägi observed that after rapid surges, many investors **expect dramatic pullbacks** and sit on the sidelines or short the market — only to watch it grind relentlessly higher. The historical data showed that:

- Post-crash recoveries often have **very shallow pullbacks** during the initial surge phase.
- Macro bears who cite unemployment, debt, or valuation metrics are often **too early** — the market can remain irrational longer than many expect.
- **Price action and moving averages are more reliable guides** than macroeconomic arguments for active traders.

---

## Key Technical Lesson

> *"Use the 10/20-day SMA on your momentum stocks as a guide. As long as price is holding above those levels, the trend is intact. Do not let negative macro narratives override what the chart is telling you."*

---

## Caution: This Is Not a Prediction

Kullamägi was explicit that this was **not a prediction** of a repeat dot-com bubble. He acknowledged that the 1999–2000 final parabolic blow-off was an extreme outlier. The purpose of studying the historical analogue was to:

1. Provide **context** for how powerful post-crash recoveries can be.
2. Help traders **manage emotions and biases** when markets surge faster than feels comfortable.
3. Reinforce the discipline of following **price action over narrative**.

---

## Practical Takeaway for Traders

- Do not fight a trending market with macro pessimism.
- Ride the trend using the **10/20 SMA as a trailing guide** for individual momentum stocks.
- Be aware that cyclical patterns repeat but do not copy exactly — use history as a reference, not a script.
- Avoid **recency bias** in either direction: neither assume every dip is a crash, nor that every rally will end in a bubble.
