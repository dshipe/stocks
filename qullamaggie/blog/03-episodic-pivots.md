# How to Master a Setup: Episodic Pivots

## Core Thesis

Most trading struggles come from **not having mastered any single setup**. Traders jump between strategies, never developing the deep pattern recognition and conviction needed to hold through normal drawdowns or to act decisively when the setup appears.

Kullamägi's prescription: go **extremely deep into one strategy**. Study hundreds — ideally thousands — of historical examples. Learn every variation, the market conditions where it thrives, and the conditions where it fails. That depth of knowledge removes hesitation and replaces it with conviction.

> *"Once you master a setup, where you know dozens of variations by heart and in which market conditions the setup works well and when it doesn't — and having conviction to hold through drawdowns — you will reach any financial goals you may have."*

---

## What Is an Episodic Pivot?

An **Episodic Pivot (EP)** is a significant, unexpected positive catalyst event that causes a stock to **gap up at least 10%** on dramatically elevated volume.

### Defining Characteristics
- **Size of gap:** Minimum 10%; best setups gap 15–30% or more.
- **Volume:** Ideally the stock trades its full average daily volume **within the first 15–20 minutes** of the session — a sign of massive institutional re-pricing.
- **Catalyst quality:** The news must be genuinely new and company-specific (not a sector/market rumor). Examples:
  - Earnings beat + raised guidance
  - FDA or regulatory approval
  - Confirmed acquisition or merger
  - Major new contract or strategic partnership
- **Prior neglect:** The best EPs come from stocks that were **previously ignored** by institutions, forcing rapid and sustained buying as funds rebuild or initiate positions.
- **Consolidation break:** Ideal EPs also coincide with the stock **breaking out of a multi-month base**, amplifying the move.

---

## How to Scan for Episodic Pivots

1. **Pre-market / after-hours screening:** Look for stocks gapping **at least 4–10%** with unusually high volume (minimum ~50k shares).
2. **At the open:** Focus on those that hold their gap and are printing **record volume in the first 5–15 minutes**.
3. **Catalyst verification:** Confirm there is a real, company-specific news event driving the move — not just market-wide momentum.

---

## Entry Strategy

| Timeframe | Entry Trigger |
|-----------|--------------|
| Aggressive | Break of the **1-minute opening range high** |
| Moderate | Break of the **5-minute opening range high** |
| Conservative | Break of the **60-minute opening range high** |

- It is **not essential** to catch the very first move. Adding throughout the session is viable as long as the strength and volume continue.
- If you miss the initial entry, watch for **intraday consolidation breakouts** or, later in the week, the stock may form a tighter base for a second-entry swing trade.

---

## Stop Loss Placement

- Place the stop at the **low of the day** or just under the opening range.
- Stop should generally be no more than **1–1.5× the average true range (ATR)**.
- A stop that is too wide relative to ATR suggests the setup is not clean enough to trade at full size — reduce size accordingly.

---

## Position Management

1. **Initial entry** at opening range break.
2. **Scale in** if the stock confirms via 5-minute or hourly highs with volume.
3. **Partial profit** after the first large-scale expansion.
4. **Hold core position** with a trailing stop or stop at break-even.
5. Watch for the stock to form a **tight base later in the week** — this can be a re-entry or add-on point for the swing.

## Types of Episodic Pivots (with Examples)

### 1. Earnings-Driven EP
The most common and reliable type. The company reports earnings that dramatically exceed expectations, often combined with raised guidance. Institutions that were underweight are forced to buy aggressively.

### 2. Regulatory / FDA Approval
Common in biotech. A drug or device gets FDA clearance, unlocking a large addressable market. These can be extremely volatile — size down accordingly.

### 3. Merger & Acquisition (M&A)
A buyout offer at a premium causes an immediate re-rating. Trade cautiously — the move is often fully priced in on the news day.

### 4. Major Partnership or Product Launch
A new contract, distribution deal, or product announcement that materially changes the growth trajectory of the company.

### 5. The "Re-Rating" EP (Best Quality)
A previously neglected stock suddenly gets noticed. No prior institutional ownership means the buying pressure is sustained for days or weeks as funds continue to build positions. These offer the best multi-day or multi-week follow-through.

---

## Market Conditions

- EPs work best in **bullish or neutral markets** — avoid trading them aggressively in confirmed downtrends where even good news gets sold.
- **Earnings season** (roughly 4× per year) creates clusters of high-quality EP opportunities.
- During strong bull phases, the best EPs can become **multi-week or multi-month runners** — size appropriately and be patient.

## Key Principle

> *"Deep study of hundreds or thousands of historical EPs builds the confidence and pattern recognition to identify high-quality setups instantly — and the conviction to hold them when they work."*
