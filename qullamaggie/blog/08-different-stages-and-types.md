# Different Stages and Types of a Company

**Author:** Kristjan Kullamägi (Qullamaggie)

## Overview

Understanding the **stage and type of a company** is fundamental to selecting stocks with the highest potential for large price moves. Kullamägi draws on the classic Peter Lynch framework (from *One Up on Wall Street*) and adapts it for momentum and swing traders who are looking for explosive, multi-day to multi-month price appreciation.

---

## The Six Types of Companies (Peter Lynch Framework)

### 1. Fast Growers
**Definition:** Companies growing earnings at **25% or more per year**, often in new or rapidly expanding markets.

**Why they matter for traders:**
- Fast growers produce the **biggest stock price appreciation** over time.
- They are the primary hunting ground for momentum and swing traders.
- Their earnings surprises (when growth exceeds already-high expectations) can trigger **Episodic Pivot** setups.

**Examples:** High-growth tech, biotech, and software companies at early-to-mid stage of their growth curve.

---

### 2. Stalwarts (Medium Growers)
**Definition:** Large, established companies growing at **10–12% annually**.

**Why they matter:**
- Less exciting for momentum trading but can still produce solid swing trade setups.
- Their size and stability makes them **less prone to the explosive moves** favoured by momentum traders.
- More relevant to long-term investors than short-term swing traders.

**Examples:** Large-cap consumer staples, established retail chains.

---

### 3. Slow Growers (Sluggards)
**Definition:** Mature companies with **minimal revenue/earnings growth**, often paying dividends to reward shareholders.

**Why they matter for traders:**
- Generally **avoid for momentum trading** — slow growers rarely produce the large price moves worth the opportunity cost.
- May be relevant for income-oriented investors but not Kullamägi's focus.

---

### 4. Cyclicals
**Definition:** Companies whose revenue and earnings **rise and fall with economic cycles** — energy, metals, commodities, airlines, autos, chemicals.

**Why they matter:**
- Can produce **enormous returns** when timed correctly at the bottom of their cycle (see: [Natural Gas Multibaggers](04-natural-gas-multibaggers.md)).
- Require understanding of the commodity/sector cycle, not just price action.
- High risk if bought at the top of the cycle — earnings collapse rapidly in downturns.

**Kullamägi's approach:** Look for deeply beaten-down cyclicals with supply/demand rebalancing thesis, confirmed by technical breakouts.

---

### 5. Turnarounds
**Definition:** Companies in **deep trouble** (declining earnings, near-bankruptcy, restructuring) that have potential to recover.

**Why they matter:**
- Successful turnarounds can produce some of the **largest percentage gains** in the market.
- Very high risk: many turnarounds fail completely.
- The best trading approach: wait for **clear technical evidence** that the turnaround is working (higher lows, base formation, breakout on volume) before committing capital.

**Examples:** Distressed energy stocks in 2020, beaten-down retail names that survive and revive.

---

### 6. Asset Plays
**Definition:** Companies with **valuable hidden assets** (real estate, patents, natural resources, subsidiaries) that the market has not properly valued.

**Why they matter:**
- Less relevant for pure momentum/swing trading.
- Can occasionally produce **catalyst-driven Episodic Pivots** when hidden value is unlocked (e.g., spin-offs, asset sales).

---

## Which Types Does Kullamägi Focus On?

| Priority | Type | Reason |
|----------|------|--------|
| **Primary** | Fast Growers | Biggest sustained price moves; best EP candidates |
| **Secondary** | Turnarounds | Can produce explosive moves; require technical confirmation |
| **Occasional** | Cyclicals | Outsized returns at cycle bottoms; supply/demand thesis |
| **Avoid** | Slow Growers, Stalwarts | Insufficient price movement for active trading |
| **Selective** | Asset Plays | Only when catalyst is imminent and confirmed |

---

## Company Stage Matters Too

Beyond the type, the **stage of the company's life cycle** affects the magnitude of potential moves:

1. **Early stage / emerging growth:** Highest potential upside; highest risk; small float can amplify moves dramatically.
2. **Mid-cycle growth:** Often the sweet spot — proven concept, still growing fast, increasing institutional interest.
3. **Late cycle / mature:** Growth decelerating; multiple compression risk; less favoured for momentum trading.
4. **Decline:** Avoid unless trading a turnaround setup with clear technical confirmation.

---

## Key Insight

> *"The biggest stock price moves come from fast growers and turnarounds — companies where the fundamental story is being either newly discovered or radically re-assessed. Understanding which type of company you are trading helps you size positions appropriately and set realistic expectations for how far the move can run."*
