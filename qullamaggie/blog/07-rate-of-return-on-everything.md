## Overview

This post draws on the landmark academic paper *"The Rate of Return on Everything, 1870–2015"* (Jorda, Knoll, Kuvshinov, Schularick, and Taylor) and applies its findings to a trader's perspective on **why equities — and specifically active trading of leading momentum stocks — offers the best risk-adjusted return opportunity** available to individual participants.

---

## Key Findings from the Academic Research

The paper studied returns across four major asset classes in 16 developed countries from 1870 to 2015:

| Asset Class | Average Real Annual Return |
|-------------|---------------------------|
| Equities (stocks) | ~7% |
| Residential Real Estate | ~7% |
| Government Bonds | ~2.5% |
| Treasury Bills (cash) | ~1% |

### Important Notes:
- **Equities and real estate** have provided remarkably similar long-run returns.
- **Bonds and cash** have dramatically underperformed in real (inflation-adjusted) terms over long periods.
- Equity returns come with **higher volatility** than real estate but with far greater **liquidity**.
- The equity premium (excess return over bonds) is real, persistent, and large.

---

## Implications for Active Traders

### Why Equities?
If passive equity investing already outperforms bonds and cash by a factor of 3–7× over decades, **active trading of the best-performing stocks** within the equity universe offers the potential for dramatically higher compounded returns — provided losses are controlled.

### The Compounding Imperative
- Small advantages compounded over many trades become enormous wealth.
- This is why Kullamägi's risk management emphasis — **never let one trade destroy your account** — is so critical: a single catastrophic loss can wipe out years of compounding.

### The Power Law of Stock Returns
Within equities, a small number of stocks produce the vast majority of returns:
- The **top 1–2% of stocks** by momentum performance account for outsized gains.
- This is why scanning for leaders and focusing only on the strongest stocks is rational, not speculative.

---

## Practical Takeaways for Traders

1. **Be in equities.** Over virtually any long time horizon, equities outperform bonds, cash, and most other assets in real terms.
2. **Focus on the leaders.** The bulk of equity returns in any given period are concentrated in a small number of stocks. Identify and trade those.
3. **Protect capital above all.** The arithmetic of compounding means that large drawdowns are extremely destructive — a 50% loss requires a 100% gain just to break even.
4. **Think in terms of compounded annual returns.** A 30% annual return compounded over 10 years turns $100,000 into ~$1.4 million. The goal is sustainable, repeated positive returns — not lottery-style all-or-nothing bets.
