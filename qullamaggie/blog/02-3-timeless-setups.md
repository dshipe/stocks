## Setup 1: The Breakout

### Concept
Stocks that make large moves rarely do so in a straight line. Instead they move in **stair-steps**: a big surge (often 20–50%+), followed by an orderly pullback and sideways consolidation, then another breakout. The goal is to catch that second (or third) leg.

### How to Find Setups
- Scan for the **top 1–2% of stocks** by price performance over the past 1, 3, and 6 months — these are current market leaders.
- Look for a prior big move in the last 1–3 months.
- Wait for an **orderly pullback with higher lows and a tightening range**, often riding the 10-, 20-, or sometimes 50-day moving average.

### Entry
- Buy on the **breakout above the consolidation**.
- Common triggers: the high of the first **1-minute**, **5-minute**, or **60-minute** opening candle.

### Risk Management
- Stop placed **just under the day's low**.
- Risk per trade: **0.25–1% of total account size**.
- Take partial profits after 3–5 days; move the stop to break-even on the remaining position and let it run.

## Setup 2: The Episodic Pivot (EP)

### Concept
An **Episodic Pivot** is a sharp, catalyst-driven gap-up of **at least 10%** on massive volume, triggered by genuinely new and significant news — an earnings beat, FDA approval, major partnership, or buyout announcement. The event forces institutions to rapidly reassess and reposition in a stock they previously ignored.

### Key Characteristics
- Gap of **10%+** at the open.
- Volume: the stock ideally trades its **entire average daily volume within the first 15–20 minutes**.
- Best EPs often break a **multi-month or multi-quarter consolidation** on the day of the catalyst.
- Neglected companies pre-event make the best candidates (no prior institutional ownership to unwind).

### Entry
- Wait for the **opening range high** to break (1-, 5-, or 60-minute candle depending on trading style and action speed).
- Add to the position as it confirms via 5-minute or hourly highs.

### Stop Loss
- Place stop at the **low of the day** or just under the opening range.
- Stop should generally not exceed **1–1.5× the average true range (ATR)**.

### Types of Episodic Pivots
1. **Earnings surprise** — most frequent and reliable.
2. **Regulatory/FDA approval** — common in biotech.
3. **Takeover rumors or confirmed buyouts**.
4. **Major partnerships or product launches**.
5. The best setups: a **neglected stock suddenly "re-rated"** by the market.

## Setup 3: The Parabolic Short (or Long on the Bounce)

### Concept
After a stock goes **vertical** — exponential price increase over multiple consecutive days, usually on expanding volume — it becomes dangerously overbought and prone to sharp, fast reversals. This setup exploits that exhaustion.

### Finding the Setup
- Identify stocks in **parabolic runs**: price going straight up for several days on big volume.
- Small-cap and low-float stocks are especially prone to parabolic moves and violent reversals.

### Entry (Short Side)
- Look for **exhaustion signals**: the stock gaps up again but then fails to make further progress intraday (reversal candle on high volume).
- Common triggers: failed break above prior highs, or a sudden drop on very high volume after a gap.

### Risk Management
- Set stop **above the intraday highs**.
- These setups are fast-moving — take profits quickly as the stock pulls back sharply.
- Do not overstay; parabolic reversals are often short-lived.

### Long on the Bounce
- Wait for the violent first leg down to complete.
- Look for a **stabilisation and bounce** off a key level (often the 20-day or 50-day MA) with volume expanding on the upside.
