# Summary: Top 10 Stock Trading Concepts by Qullamaggie

**Author:** Kristjan Kullamägi (Qullamaggie)  
**Source:** Synthesised from all blog posts, tweetstorms, videos, and public content.

These are the **10 most repeated and foundational concepts** across all of Kullamägi's publicly available content.

---

## Concept 1: Master One Setup Deeply Before Moving to Another

The single most repeated theme across all of Kullamägi's content. He argues that the reason most traders fail is that they never go deep enough on any single pattern to develop true conviction and automatic pattern recognition.

**The prescription:** Pick one setup (e.g., the breakout or the episodic pivot). Study **hundreds of historical examples**. Learn every variation: when it works, when it fails, what the best and worst versions look like. Only after mastering one setup does adding a second one make sense.

> *"Once you master a setup — knowing dozens of variations by heart and understanding in which market conditions it works — you will reach any financial goals you may have."*

---

## Concept 2: Focus on the Strongest Stocks (Relative Strength)

Always trade stocks in the **top 1–2% by price performance** over the past 1, 3, and 6 months. These are the current market leaders — the stocks that institutions are actively buying. Trading leading stocks in leading sectors dramatically increases the probability of large moves.

**Key principle:** If the market is down and your stock is flat or up, that **relative strength** is an extremely powerful signal. Those are the stocks that will lead the next leg up.

## Concept 3: Three Core Setups — Breakout, Episodic Pivot, Parabolic Short

Kullamägi's entire trading system reduces to three repeatable setups:

1. **Breakout:** Buy when a consolidating leader breaks above its range on high volume.
2. **Episodic Pivot (EP):** Buy a stock that gaps up 10%+ on a genuine catalyst when it breaks its opening range high.
3. **Parabolic Short:** Short (or buy the bounce from) a stock that has gone vertical and is showing exhaustion.

These three setups work across decades, markets, and countries because they exploit permanent structural inefficiencies: institutional accumulation, forced re-ratings, and crowd psychology exhaustion.

## Concept 4: Risk Management Is the Foundation — Not an Afterthought

No trading concept is repeated more frequently in Kullamägi's content. The rules are simple but non-negotiable:

- **Risk per trade:** 0.25–1% of total account equity. No exceptions.
- **Maximum single position overnight:** 30% of account.
- **Stop loss placement:** Defined before entering the trade. Never moved wider.
- **Never average down** on a losing position.
- **One large loss can erase months of gains** — the math of drawdowns is brutal. A 50% loss requires 100% gain to recover.

> *"Every trader will take big losses. The difference between professionals and amateurs is that professionals keep their losses within planned limits."*

---

## Concept 5: Cut Losses Quickly; Let Winners Run

The fundamental asymmetry of profitable trading: **small, frequent losses** are acceptable; large losses are catastrophic. Meanwhile, a single large winner can pay for many small losers.

This requires consistent discipline to:
- Exit losing trades at the **pre-defined stop** without hesitation.
- **Not exit winning trades early** out of fear — let the trend do its work.
- Scale out partially at targets while maintaining a core position in strong trends.

The natural human tendency is the exact opposite — hold losers hoping they recover; sell winners out of fear they'll reverse. Overcoming this bias is the core psychological challenge of trading.

## Concept 6: Preparation Before the Market Open Is Non-Negotiable

Every profitable trading day begins **before the market opens**. Kullamägi's preparation routine:

1. Build and review a **watchlist** of the strongest stocks showing potential setups.
2. Set **price alerts** at key breakout or entry levels.
3. Define the **game plan** for each stock: entry trigger, stop-loss level, number of shares.
4. Know exactly what you will do if the stock triggers — no improvisation during trading hours.

Traders who wing it during market hours are making reactive, emotional decisions under time pressure. Traders who have a plan are executing, not deciding.

---

## Concept 7: Market Context Determines Aggressiveness

Not all market environments are equally productive. Kullamägi explicitly identifies:

- **Strong bull market:** Be aggressive; size up on A+ setups; hold positions longer.
- **Choppy / unclear market:** Reduce size dramatically; prefer cash; raise the bar for setups.
- **Bear market:** Preserve capital; consider shorting parabolic stocks; do not fight the downtrend.

Using the **NASDAQ or S&P 500 moving averages** (10-day, 20-day, 50-day) as a guide to overall market health is a simple and effective filter.

---

## Concept 8: Understand Company Types to Assess Potential

Drawing on Peter Lynch's framework, Kullamägi distinguishes which company types offer the biggest potential stock moves:

- **Fast growers** (25%+ earnings growth): The primary focus for momentum traders.
- **Turnarounds**: Can produce explosive moves; require technical confirmation before entry.
- **Cyclicals**: Outsized returns at cycle bottoms when supply/demand rebalances.
- **Slow growers and stalwarts**: Generally avoid for active trading.

The best episodic pivots and breakouts come from **fast growers being newly discovered or re-rated** by institutions.

---

## Concept 9: Trading Psychology — Control Emotion, Follow Process

Kullamägi returns repeatedly to the psychological dimension of trading:

- **Revenge trading** (immediately trying to recover a loss) is one of the most destructive behaviours.
- **Overtrading** — forcing trades when no good setup exists — erodes capital and confidence.
- **Attachment to positions** clouds judgment; your P&L should not influence your market analysis.
- After a large loss, the correct response is to **step away and reset** — not to trade more aggressively.
- Build a **systematic process** so that decisions are made by rules, not emotions.

The goal is to make trading boring and mechanical — the excitement of "gambling" is a warning sign, not a feature.

---

## Concept 10: Build a Chartbook and Study Obsessively

Kullamägi's development as a trader came through **massive, systematic study** of historical charts — thousands of them, across different setups, outcomes, and market conditions.

**The process:**
1. Study 1,000+ historical examples of your target setup.
2. Categorise them: what worked, what failed, what made the best ones different.
3. Review regularly, including your own recent trades (wins and losses equally).
4. Build **mental pattern recognition** that makes identifying true setups automatic.

This chartbook approach — more than any tool, screener, or indicator — is what Kullamägi credits for building his edge. It cannot be shortcut.

> *"After studying 500 episodic pivot charts, you'll immediately know the difference between a true EP and a fake one — without needing a checklist."*
