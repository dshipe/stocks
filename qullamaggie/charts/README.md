## Setup 1: The Classic Breakout

### What the Chart Looks Like
```
Price
 |           ___/‾‾‾ (Breakout leg)
 |          /
 |_________/ ← Consolidation/base (10-30 days)
 |   ↑
 |  Prior big move (20-50%+)
 |_____________________________ Time
```

### Key Visual Characteristics
- **Prior move:** Stock up 20–100%+ in the last 1–3 months (the first leg).
- **Base formation:** Price contracts into a tight sideways range with declining volume ("drying up").
- **Moving averages:** Price rides the 10-day or 20-day SMA during the consolidation — these act as a floor.
- **Breakout candle:** A large-range candle on **2×+ average daily volume** breaks above the consolidation high.
- **Ideal entry:** Just above the high of the first 1-minute, 5-minute, or 60-minute candle on breakout day.

## Setup 2: The Episodic Pivot (EP)

### What the Chart Looks Like
```
Price
 |              |→ Gap up 10–30%+ on news
 |           ___↑___
 |          |       \___  (Opening range, then breakout above it)
 |__________|
 |← Multi-month base / prior neglect
 |___________________________________ Time
```

### Key Visual Characteristics
- **Large gap:** Price opens 10–30% (or more) above the prior close due to a catalyst.
- **Pre-market volume:** Significant volume before the open (ideally 50k+ shares).
- **Opening range:** First 1–5 minutes of trading define the range; the breakout above this range is the entry.
- **Volume explosion:** Full average daily volume often prints in the first 15–20 minutes.
- **Follow-through:** The stock holds its gap and closes near the highs of the day on the biggest volume ever.

### Types of Catalyst Events
- Earnings beat + raised guidance
- FDA or regulatory approval
- Merger / acquisition announcement
- Major new contract or partnership
- CEO change / activist investor

## Setup 3: The High Tight Flag

### What the Chart Looks Like
```
Price
 |          /‾ (Flag — tight consolidation after parabolic move)
 |         /__|
 |        /
 |       /← Pole (100–400% move in 4–8 weeks)
 |______/
 |__________________________________ Time
```

### Key Visual Characteristics
- **Pole:** Stock runs up **100–400%** in just 4–8 weeks — an unusually powerful and compressed move.
- **Flag:** Price then consolidates in a **tight range** (typically <25% retracement of the pole) for 1–4 weeks.
- **Volume:** Volume contracts sharply during the flag formation.
- **Breakout:** Price breaks above the flag on renewed high volume — the setup for the next leg up.

## Setup 4: The First Pullback to Key Moving Average

### What the Chart Looks Like
```
Price
 |               ← First touch of 10/20 SMA after breakout
 |      _____/‾‾‾\___/‾‾‾
 |     /                 → Entry: Buy the first pullback to MA
 |    /← Breakout
 |___/_____________________________ Time
     10/20 SMA acting as support
```

### Key Visual Characteristics
- Stock has already had a strong initial breakout (Setup 1 or EP).
- Price pulls back to the **10-day or 20-day SMA** for the first time after the move.
- Volume contracts during the pullback.
- Price bounces off the moving average on lighter-than-average volume — then expands again on the move higher.
- **Entry:** Buy the bounce off the 10/20 SMA with a stop just below the MA.

## Setup 5: The Parabolic Exhaustion (Short)

### What the Chart Looks Like
```
Price
 |           /| ← Gap up but fails intraday — potential short entry
 |          / |
 |         /  ↓ Reversal on high volume
 |        /
 |       /← Vertical run (multiple consecutive up days, expanding volume)
 |______/
 |__________________________________ Time
```

### Key Visual Characteristics
- Stock has run **straight up** for several consecutive days, each close near the high.
- Volume is **expanding** on each up day (unsustainable).
- A **gap-up day that reverses intraday** — especially on the highest volume of the run — is the exhaustion signal.
- The failed move signals that buyers are exhausted and sellers are taking control.

## Tips for Identifying High-Quality Setups (Any Type)

| Characteristic | What to Look For |
|---------------|-----------------|
| **Relative strength** | Stock outperforming the broader market significantly |
| **Volume signature** | Volume much higher than average on the setup day; drying up on consolidation days |
| **Price proximity to highs** | Near 52-week or all-time highs (not in a downtrend) |
| **Tight price action** | Narrow daily ranges during consolidation = institutional accumulation |
| **Moving average support** | Price respecting the 10-, 20-, or 50-day SMA as floor |
| **Catalyst quality (EPs)** | Fresh, company-specific news that forces institutional reassessment |
| **Market conditions** | Setups work best when the overall market (NASDAQ, S&P) is in an uptrend |

## How to Build a Chart Reference Library

Kullamägi repeatedly emphasises building a **personal chartbook** of historical setups:

1. **Study 1,000+ charts** of past winners across your target setups.
2. **Tag each chart** with setup type, entry trigger, outcome, and lessons.
3. **Review regularly** — especially losing setups — to understand failure modes.
4. **Build mental pattern recognition** so that identifying high-quality setups becomes instinctive rather than analytical.

*"After studying 500 episodic pivot charts, you'll immediately know the difference between a true EP and a fake one — without needing a checklist."*
