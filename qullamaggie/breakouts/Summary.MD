# Qullamaggie Breakout Setup — Summary

> Based on Kristjan Kullamägi's (Qullamaggie) momentum breakout trading methodology.

---

## The Five Most Repeated Concepts for Identifying a Breakout Setup

### 1. Explosive Prior Move (The Launch)
The most consistently repeated concept across all Qullamaggie resources: a valid breakout candidate **must first have had a powerful prior move** — typically a 30–100%+ gain in a short timeframe (1–8 weeks). This prior surge establishes the stock as a market leader, confirms institutional interest, and creates the momentum necessary for a successful continuation breakout.

**Key signals:**
- Stock gained 30% or more in under 2 months
- Move was driven by a clear catalyst (earnings beat, FDA approval, contract win, product launch)
- Relative volume during the surge was significantly above average (2–5x normal)
- The stock outperforms its sector and the broad market

---

### 2. Tight, Orderly Consolidation Near the Highs (The Base)
After the explosive move, the stock must form a **tight, shallow base** near its recent highs. This is the "resting phase" before the next move. The best setups have an extremely narrow price range — ideally less than 10% from base high to low.

**Key signals:**
- Price consolidation of 1–8 weeks (shorter bases after bigger moves are acceptable)
- High-to-low range within the base is under 10–15%
- Price action is orderly — no wide, choppy swings
- Stock holds above its 10-day and 20-day moving averages
- Base forms as a recognizable pattern: flat base, high-tight flag, pennant, or rectangle

---

### 3. Volume Contraction During the Base (Seller Exhaustion)
The third universal concept is **volume drying up** significantly during the consolidation phase. Declining volume signals that sellers have been exhausted and that remaining holders are strong hands unwilling to sell. This sets the stage for the breakout surge.

**Key signals:**
- Daily volume shrinks to 50% or less of the stock's average daily volume during consolidation
- No high-volume distribution days (large down-volume days) within the base
- Volume contraction is visible and consistent — each contraction in the VCP pattern is smaller than the previous
- Occasionally, a "shakeout" day with temporary volume spike is acceptable if price recovers quickly

---

### 4. Breakout on Expanding Volume (The Trigger)
The breakout itself must be confirmed by a **surge in volume** — this is what distinguishes a true breakout from a false one. Institutional buying shows up as dramatically increased volume on the day price breaks above the base high.

**Key signals:**
- Price breaks above the high of the consolidation base (the pivot point)
- Breakout volume is 40–100%+ above the average daily volume
- Breakout candle is strong: closes near the high of the day
- The move above resistance is decisive — not hesitant or choppy
- Can enter intraday on the break of the opening range high (ORH) or end of day

---

### 5. Strict Risk Management and Asymmetric Risk/Reward
Perhaps the most defining rule of Qullamaggie's approach: **control losses ruthlessly and let winners run**. He expects a win rate of only 20–35%, but makes the strategy highly profitable by keeping losses tiny and riding the big winners. The stop-loss must be placed with surgical precision.

**Key signals:**
- Initial stop loss placed just below the consolidation low or below the breakout day's low
- Risk per trade = 0.25%–1% of total capital (typically 0.5%)
- Stop distance should not exceed 1x Average True Range (ATR)
- Minimum reward-to-risk ratio: 2:1 (aim for 3:1 or higher)
- Never hold through earnings if the position was entered after the catalyst

## When to Set an Alert and Buy the Breakout

### Pre-Breakout Alert Setup
Set a price alert on the stock at the **pivot price** — the high of the consolidation base. This is the exact price where the breakout triggers.

**Alert criteria:**
1. Stock has completed the prior explosive move (30%+)
2. Stock is currently in a tight consolidation (volume is declining, range is narrow)
3. The consolidation has lasted at least 5–10 trading days
4. Price is near the top of the base, within 2–3% of the pivot
5. The broad market is in an uptrend (confirm with S&P 500 / NASDAQ trend)

### Buying the Breakout — Entry Rules
**Option A: Intraday Entry (Aggressive)**
- Wait for the market to open
- Watch the stock's opening range (first 5–15 minutes)
- Enter when price breaks above the **Opening Range High (ORH)** with strong volume in the first 30 minutes
- Place stop below the opening range low (or the day's low)
- This method captures the full move but requires active monitoring

**Option B: End-of-Day Entry (Conservative)**
- Wait until 30–60 minutes before market close
- Confirm price is breaking above the pivot with volume > 150% of average
- Buy near the close if the breakout is confirmed and holding
- Place stop below the consolidation base low

**Entry Filters (must be true to enter):**
- [ ] Volume is expanding — at least 1.5–2x average by midday
- [ ] Price is above the pivot/base high and holding
- [ ] Broad market is not in a severe downtrend
- [ ] No earnings within the next 3 trading days
- [ ] Stock price is above $5 and average daily volume > 300,000 shares
- [ ] ADR% (Average Daily Range) is above 3–4%

**Position Sizing Formula:**
```
Dollar Risk = Account Size × 0.5% (risk parameter)
Share Count = Dollar Risk ÷ (Entry Price – Stop Price)
Max Position = Account Size × 20% (never exceed for overnight holds)
```

## When to Take Profit and Sell a Breakout

### Partial Profit-Taking (Scale Out)
Do NOT sell the entire position at once. Use a tiered exit strategy:

**First Sale (1/3 to 1/2 of position):**
- Sell when the trade has moved **2–3× your initial risk** (2R–3R)
- Typically occurs within 3–5 trading days of breakout
- Example: Entered at $50 with $2 risk → sell first tranche at $54–$56
- After first sale, move stop-loss to **breakeven** (your entry price)

**Trailing the Remainder:**
- Use the **10-day moving average** as your trailing stop
- Sell remaining shares when the stock **closes below the 10-day MA**
- Alternatively, trail using the **20-day MA** for larger-trend trades
- This allows capturing extended multi-week or multi-month moves

### Hard Sell Signals (Exit Immediately)
Exit the full position without hesitation if:
1. **Stop loss is hit** — price closes below the consolidation base low
2. **Failed breakout** — stock reverses back into the base (close back below pivot)
3. **Climax top signals**: Extremely wide spread up day on 3–5x volume after an extended run (exhaustion move)
4. **Earnings risk**: Approaching earnings date without a significant cushion
5. **Market breaks down**: Index (S&P/NASDAQ) drops sharply and confirms downtrend
6. **High-volume reversal**: Massive up-open followed by reversal to close down on huge volume (key reversal day)

### Profit Target Ladder Summary

| Stage | Trigger | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Initial breakout holds | Price stays above pivot after buy | Hold, maintain stop below base |
| 2R reached | Price up 2× your risk from entry | Sell 1/3 to 1/2, move stop to breakeven |
| 3R reached | Price up 3× your risk | Sell another 1/4, tighten trail |
| Extended run | Price riding 10-day MA | Hold remainder, trail with 10-day MA |
| Exit signal | Close below 10-day MA or stop hit | Exit remaining position |

### What NOT to Do
- **Never** add to a losing position
- **Never** move your stop wider to avoid being stopped out
- **Never** hold through earnings hoping for a good result
- **Never** let a 2R winner turn into a full loss
