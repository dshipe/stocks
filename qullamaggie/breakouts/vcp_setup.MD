# Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP) — Setup Guide

> The VCP is the most precise and reliable version of the Qullamaggie breakout setup.
> It combines elements from Mark Minervini's work with Qullamaggie's execution methodology.

---

## What Is the VCP?

The **Volatility Contraction Pattern** (VCP) was originally described by Mark Minervini and adopted by Kristjan Kullamägi as one of his preferred entry setups. It describes a base where price volatility progressively *contracts* — each pullback is smaller than the last, volume declines with each contraction, and the stock sets up for a very tight, low-risk breakout entry.

The VCP demonstrates that as the base matures:
- Sellers are gradually being exhausted (declining supply)
- Buyers are absorbing shares at higher and higher lows (increasing demand)
- The eventual breakout happens from a position of extreme tightness — where the stock can move significantly with minimal overhead resistance

## The VCP Structure

### Visual Anatomy
```
VCP — DETAILED STRUCTURE
=========================

         INITIAL HIGH
              *
             * *
            *   *
           *     *
          *       *       ← Contraction 1 (C1): 20% depth
         *         *
        *     C1    * * * *     ← First recovery
       *                   *
      *                     *   ← C2: 12% depth
     *                       * *
    *           C2              *  * *   ← Second recovery
   *                                 *
  *                                   *  ← C3: 7% depth
 *                                     * *
*                       C3                *
                                           *  *  ← Tight pivot (C4: 3–4%)
                                             * * ← Breakout line
                                              * * * * * / ← BREAKOUT

C1 > C2 > C3 > C4 (each contraction DECREASING in size)
V1 > V2 > V3 > V4 (volume DECREASING with each contraction)
```

### Key Measurements

| Contraction | Depth | Volume Level | Duration |
|-------------|-------|--------------|----------|
| C1 (First) | 15–25% | High (after prior move) | 2–3 weeks |
| C2 | 10–15% | Moderate-low | 1–2 weeks |
| C3 | 5–10% | Low | 5–10 days |
| C4 (Pivot) | 2–5% | Very low (50%+ below avg) | 3–7 days |

## Why the VCP Works (Psychology)

**Phase 1 (C1):** Weak holders ("paper hands") sell after the initial surge. Volume is still elevated. This is the largest contraction.

**Phase 2 (C2):** Intermediate-term sellers exit. Volume falls. Buyers begin absorbing more supply. The contraction is smaller because fewer shares are available at discount levels.

**Phase 3 (C3):** Only strong holders remain. Volume is very low. The stock barely moves down anymore — sellers are almost gone.

**Phase 4 (C4 — Pivot):** The tightest area. All weak sellers have been shaken out. The remaining float is held by committed long-term holders. Even a small influx of buyers can send the stock sharply higher.

**Breakout:** When institutional buying returns on news, earnings, or a sector catalyst, there is almost no supply to absorb — the stock surges rapidly.

## VCP Entry Rules

### Identifying the Pivot Point
The pivot point is the **high of the final contraction (C4)**. This is your breakout line. Set a price alert here.

**How to find it:**
1. Draw a horizontal line at the high of the tightest consolidation area
2. This is where you will place your buy-stop order

### Entry Checklist
```
VCP ENTRY CHECKLIST
====================
[ ] Stock had a 30%+ prior explosive move
[ ] At least 2–3 progressive contractions visible
[ ] Each contraction is smaller than the prior
[ ] Volume declined with each contraction
[ ] Volume in the final contraction is ≤ 50% of 20-day avg
[ ] 10-day MA is above 20-day MA
[ ] Price is in the upper 25% of its 52-week range
[ ] No earnings within 3 trading days
[ ] Broad market is in an uptrend
[ ] Risk/reward is ≥ 2:1
```

### Buy Order Placement
- **Option A (Intraday):** Break of the pivot on the 1-min, 5-min, or 60-min Opening Range High
- **Option B (Daily):** Buy-stop order 5–10 cents above the pivot high
- **Option C (End of Day):** Buy during the last 30 minutes if the stock has broken the pivot and volume is confirming

## VCP Stop Loss Placement

### Rule: Stop below the FINAL contraction low
- Find the lowest price in the C4 (final contraction) area
- Subtract a small buffer (0.5%) to avoid premature stop-outs on noise
- This should be no more than 5–7% below your entry

### ATR Rule
- If (Entry – Stop) > 1× ATR(14), the stop is too wide
- In this case, skip the trade or wait for a tighter setup to develop

### Stop Adjustment After Entry
- Once the stock has moved 1R in your favor → move stop to 0.5R above entry
- Once the stock has moved 2R → move stop to breakeven
- After that: trail with the 10-day MA

## VCP Position Sizing Example

```
Example: A stock with a clean 3-contraction VCP

Account size: $50,000
Risk per trade: 0.5% = $250

Pivot high (entry): $75.00
Final contraction low (C4 low): $71.50
Buffer: $71.25 (stop)

Risk per share: $75.00 – $71.25 = $3.75
Position size: $250 / $3.75 = 66 shares
Position value: 66 × $75 = $4,950 (9.9% of account — good)

Target 1 (2R): $75 + (2 × $3.75) = $82.50 → Sell 33 shares
Target 2 (3R): $75 + (3 × $3.75) = $86.25 → Sell 17 shares
Remainder: 16 shares, trail with 10-day MA
```

## Common VCP Mistakes to Avoid

### 1. Buying Too Early (Before the Pivot)
- Many traders buy during the contraction, thinking it's "cheap"
- This is wrong — the base may continue to develop, and the stop may be too far away
- Wait for the actual pivot break

### 2. Using a Stop That's Too Wide
- If the stop is wider than 1× ATR, the trade is already high-risk
- Skip the trade and wait for a cleaner setup with a tighter final contraction

### 3. Ignoring Volume on the Breakout
- A VCP without volume expansion on breakout is a red flag
- Volume must surge — sellers who were absent during the base must now be *overwhelmed* by buyers

### 4. Trading VCPs in Bear Markets
- VCPs fail far more often when the broad market is in a downtrend
- Always check that the S&P 500 and NASDAQ are in confirmed uptrends

### 5. Confusing a Choppy Base with a VCP
- A true VCP has *orderly* contractions with clear downsloping volume
- A choppy base has erratic swings and irregular volume — this is NOT a VCP

## VCP in Different Timeframes

| Timeframe | Duration of Contractions | Stop Distance | Best For |
|-----------|--------------------------|---------------|---------|
| Daily (standard) | Days to weeks | 3–8% | Swing traders |
| Weekly | Weeks to months | 8–15% | Position traders |
| 60-minute | Hours to days | 1–3% | Active day traders |

Qullamaggie primarily uses the **daily chart** for VCP setup identification.

## VCP Grading System (Setup Quality)

Grade your setups before trading them. Only take A or B grades.

| Grade | Criteria |
|-------|----------|
| **A+** | 4+ contractions, each clearly smaller; volume almost zero at pivot; strong prior move 60%+; institutional catalyst visible; market in uptrend |
| **A** | 3+ contractions; volume drying up well; strong prior move 40%+; market neutral to bullish |
| **B** | 2–3 contractions; decent volume contraction; 30%+ prior move |
| **C** | 2 contractions but choppy; volume not clearly declining; only take if other factors exceptional |
| **F** | Choppy, no clear contractions, or volume expanding during base → DO NOT TRADE |

## Real Stock Examples

### SHOP (Shopify) — October 2020
- C1: 22% contraction, weeks 1–2
- C2: 14% contraction, weeks 3–4  
- C3: 6% contraction, week 5–6
- Volume declined with each stage
- Breakout above final pivot on 3× volume
- Result: +38% in 6 weeks

### DDOG (Datadog) — 2021
- 4-contraction VCP over 8 weeks
- Final contraction was 4% deep
- Volume near zero before breakout
- Earnings catalyst confirmed the breakout
- Result: +50%+ move from pivot
