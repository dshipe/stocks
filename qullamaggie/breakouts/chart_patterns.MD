# Qullamaggie Breakout Setup — Chart Patterns Reference

> Detailed descriptions of each chart pattern used in the Qullamaggie breakout methodology.
> Use this as a visual reference guide for pattern recognition.

---

## Pattern 1: High-Tight Flag (HTF)

The High-Tight Flag is Qullamaggie's favorite pattern for its explosive potential.

### Definition
A stock that has surged **50–100%+ in under 8 weeks**, followed by a brief (1–4 week) shallow pullback of less than 20%. The "flag" is the consolidation portion; the "pole" is the prior surge.

### Visual Structure
```
HIGH-TIGHT FLAG PATTERN
========================

Price
 ^
 |              ***
 |          ****   **
 |        **         ***  ***   ← FLAG (consolidation, <20% deep)
 |      **               *
 |    **            ← POLE (50–100%+ surge)
 |  **
 | *
 |*
 +---------------------------------> Time
   Weeks 1-4      Weeks 5-8

Volume
 ||||||||||||          Volume HIGH during pole
         ||||||
              ||||
               |||
                |   ← Volume LOW during flag
```

### Criteria
- Pole: +50% to +100% gain in 2–8 weeks
- Flag depth: less than 20% pullback from the pole high
- Flag duration: 5–20 trading days
- Flag volume: 40–60% below average (the drier, the better)
- No heavy distribution days (volume spikes on down days) in the flag

### Entry
- Buy on the break above the flag's upper trendline (resistance)
- Confirm with volume ≥ 1.5× average on breakout day

### Stop Loss
- Below the flag low
- Or: 5–8% below the breakout entry

### Target
- Measure the height of the pole and project it upward from the breakout point
- Typically 30–50%+ gain potential

### Real Examples
- ANF (Abercrombie & Fitch) — 2023 earnings-driven HTF (+100% prior, +18% post)
- BKKT (Bakkt Holdings) — 2025 new management catalyst (+70% flag breakout)
- SMCI (Super Micro Computer) — 2024 AI infrastructure theme

## Pattern 2: Flat Base / Rectangle

A flat base is the most common continuation pattern and is highly reliable when tight.

### Definition
After a strong prior move, the stock enters a **sideways trading range** where price oscillates between clear horizontal support and resistance. The range should be less than 10% wide.

### Visual Structure
```
FLAT BASE PATTERN
=================

Price
 ^
 |         ===================  ← Resistance (the breakout line)
 |   *    * *                 * *
 |  * *  *   *               *   *
 | *   **     * * * * * * * *     ←  Flat Base (tight range)
 |*             ===================  ← Support
 |
 +------------------------------------> Time
  Prior Move     2–8 Weeks of Base     Breakout →

Volume
 ||||||||||||      ||||||||   |||||||||||||||||||||  ← HIGH on breakout
             ||||||    ||||||
              | |  | |  | |   ← LOW in base (good!)
```

### Criteria
- Prior move: 30%+ gain
- Base range: less than 10% (high minus low divided by high)
- Base duration: 2–8 weeks (10–40 trading days)
- Volume contracts steadily throughout the base
- 10-day MA stays above 20-day MA inside the base
- No close below the 50-day MA

### Entry
- Buy on the break above the flat base resistance line
- Confirm with volume expansion (ideally 2× or more)

### Stop Loss
- Below the flat base support level (the base low)
- Tight stop = small risk

### Target
- Trail with the 10-day moving average after entry
- Partial profit at 2–3× initial risk

### Real Examples
- NVDA (Nvidia) — 2020 flat base before earnings catalyst breakout
- SHOP (Shopify) — Multiple flat bases throughout 2020 run
- TSLA (Tesla) — 2020 handle breakout, 2021 flat base breakout at $800

## Pattern 3: Volatility Contraction Pattern (VCP)

The VCP is a more advanced pattern that shows progressive tightening — a series of contractions with declining depth and volume.

### Definition
A series of **2–6 price contractions** (pullbacks within the base), each one smaller than the last, with volume declining at each stage. The final contraction is very tight (3–5%), creating an extremely low-risk entry point.

### Visual Structure
```
VCP (VOLATILITY CONTRACTION PATTERN)
=====================================

Price
 ^
 |     *                                     ← Pivot / Breakout above here
 |    * *                                   /
 |   *   *      *                          /
 |  *     *    * *       *               /
 | *       *  *   *     * *     *       /← Final tight pivot
 |*         **     *   *   *   * *    */
 |           ↑      * *     * *   * */
 |          C1       ↑       ↑   ↑↑
 |                   C2      C3  C4 ← Each contraction smaller
 +-------------------------------------------> Time

 C1: 20% deep contraction | C2: 12% | C3: 7% | C4: 3-4% (pivot)

Volume
 ||||||||||||
        |||||||
              |||||
                 |||
                   | ← Almost no volume at final contraction
                   |
                   ||||||||||||  ← SURGE on breakout
```

### Criteria
- Start with a 30%+ prior move
- Base contains 2–6 contractions
- Each successive contraction: smaller depth + lower volume
- Typical progression: 20% → 12% → 7% → 4% (or similar)
- Final contraction (pivot area) should be 3–7% wide
- Volume at final contraction: 60–80% below average

### Entry
- Buy the break above the final (tightest) contraction high
- This is the "pivot point"
- Volume must surge on breakout

### Stop Loss
- Just below the final contraction low
- Often very tight (3–5% from entry)
- This creates excellent risk/reward

### Target
- At least 2–3× the risk from the stop
- Can be a large move (50–100%+) if the prior move was large

### Real Examples
- SHOP (Shopify) — 3-contraction VCP in October 2020
- Any stock consolidating after a 100%+ move will often show VCP characteristics
- Mark Minervini's original examples: dozens in his Trade Like a Stock Market Wizard books

## Pattern 4: Pennant / Symmetrical Triangle

A pennant forms when the consolidation creates converging trendlines — lower highs and higher lows meeting at an apex.

### Definition
After a sharp move, price action tightens into a **triangle shape** with both upper resistance and lower support converging. Volume contracts as the apex approaches, and the breakout follows in the direction of the prior trend (continuation).

### Visual Structure
```
PENNANT PATTERN
===============

Price
 ^
 |                    /\
 |                   /  \           ← Breakout above upper trendline
 |                  /    \         /
 |             ----/      \-------/
 |            /    --------\----- ← Converging lines
 |      *****               *---
 |  ****                          ← Upper trendline declining
 | *    lower trendline rising
 |*
 +------------------------------------> Time
  Strong move   Pennant (convergence)  Breakout

Volume
 ||||||||||||||||||
               |||||
                 |||
                  ||
                   |  ← Declining to near-zero at apex
                   ||||||||||||   ← Expansion on breakout
```

### Criteria
- Prior move: 30%+ sharp surge (flagpole)
- Pennant: series of lower highs and higher lows
- Duration: 5–20 trading days
- Volume contracts steadily toward the apex
- Breakout above upper descending trendline with volume

### Entry
- Buy on the break above the upper trendline of the pennant
- Volume confirmation required

### Stop Loss
- Below the pennant low (lower trendline)

## Pattern 5: Cup-With-Handle (CWH)

A longer-term base pattern (weeks to months) featuring a rounded bottom and a brief "handle" before the breakout.

### Definition
The stock declines from a high in a **rounded, U-shaped** fashion (the cup), then forms a brief consolidation (the handle) before breaking out to new highs. Popularized by William O'Neil.

### Visual Structure
```
CUP-WITH-HANDLE PATTERN
=========================

Price
 ^                              * ← BREAKOUT here
 |    *                        *  handle high
 |     *                  ****  ← Handle (tight, <10%)
 |      *                **
 |       *          *****
 |        **      **         ← Cup bottom (rounded)
 |          ******
 +--------------------------------------------> Time
   Left side    Cup bottom    Right side  Handle  Breakout

Volume
 ||||||||||||                                 ||||||||||||||
             |||||||||||||||||||||||||||
               ← LOW during cup/handle ←          ← HIGH on breakout
```

### Criteria
- Cup depth: 15–35% (can be deeper in highly volatile stocks)
- Cup duration: 7 weeks to 65 weeks (longer is often more reliable)
- Handle depth: less than 10–12% (shallower is better)
- Handle duration: 1–4 weeks
- Volume dries up in the handle: 40–60% below average
- Handle should form in the upper half of the cup

### Entry
- Buy on the break above the handle high (the "pivot")
- Need 40–50%+ above-average volume

### Stop Loss
- Below the handle low (typically 5–8% below entry)

### Real Examples
- TSLA (Tesla) — July 2020 (modified cup with handle)
- AAPL (Apple) — Multiple CWH patterns over its history
- AMZN (Amazon) — 2016–2017 multiple CWH patterns

## Pattern 6: Episodic Pivot (EP)

The Episodic Pivot is not a traditional base pattern — it's a gap-up on news that itself becomes the setup.

### Definition
A stock **gaps up 8–10%+ on a major catalyst** (earnings, FDA, acquisition), opening at a new level and holding those gains throughout the day. The gap and the first day's range itself becomes the "base" from which to trade.

### Visual Structure
```
EPISODIC PIVOT PATTERN
=======================

Price
 ^
 |                      /→ Continuation after EP
 |               ------/ ← 1-5 day mini-consolidation
 |              /
 |  ←  GAP  →  ← 8-15%+ gap up on news/catalyst
 |  (old range)
 |  ***** ← Stock was range-bound here
 |  *****
 +--------------------------------------------> Time
   Pre-catalyst   Gap Day    Mini-base   Breakout

Volume
 |||||  ← Normal volume
        |||||||||||||||||||||||  ← MASSIVE volume on gap day (2-5x ADV)
                         |||||||  ← Declining during mini-base
                               ||||||||||||  ← Expansion on continuation
```

### Criteria
- Gap size: 8%+ above prior close
- Volume on gap day: 2–5× average daily volume (more is better)
- Price holds the opening range all day (no reversal)
- Volume on gap day should exceed 100% of ADV within first 20 minutes

### Entry (Two Methods)
- **Method 1 (Intraday):** Buy the break of the 5-minute Opening Range High (ORH) with stop at the 5-minute Opening Range Low (ORL)
- **Method 2 (Next day/days):** Wait for a 1–5 day mini-consolidation and buy the break of that mini-base high

### Stop Loss
- Method 1: Below the opening range low
- Method 2: Below the mini-consolidation low
- Maximum stop distance: 1× ATR from entry

### Real Examples
- NVDA August 2020 (earnings EP + continuation)
- PLTR November 2024 (earnings EP + multi-week run)
- Any high-quality stock with a major earnings gap

## How to Identify the Pattern Type Quickly

Ask these questions in order:
1. Was there a 50%+ move in under 8 weeks? → Likely **High-Tight Flag**
2. Is the base perfectly flat and narrow (<10%)? → **Flat Base**
3. Are there multiple contracting pullbacks with declining volume? → **VCP**
4. Is the base triangular (converging highs and lows)? → **Pennant**
5. Is there a U-shape followed by a short handle? → **Cup-with-Handle**
6. Was there a sudden 8%+ gap up on major news? → **Episodic Pivot**

In practice, many setups combine features of multiple patterns — the VCP is often seen within a flat base or cup-with-handle.
