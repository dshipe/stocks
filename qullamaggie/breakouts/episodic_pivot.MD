# Episodic Pivot Setup — Deep Dive

> The Episodic Pivot (EP) is a specific breakout variant within the Qullamaggie methodology.
> It focuses on catalyst-driven gap-ups that force rapid institutional revaluation of a stock.

---

## What Is an Episodic Pivot?

An **Episodic Pivot** occurs when a stock suddenly becomes a completely different investment proposition due to a powerful, unexpected catalyst. This forces institutional funds — mutual funds, hedge funds, ETFs — to rapidly acquire shares, creating a powerful, sustained uptrend.

The term was popularized by Kristjan Kullamägi (Qullamaggie) and draws from the concept of a stock "pivoting" to a new price level from which it doesn't look back.

### The Core Idea
- Before the catalyst: the stock is under the radar, often range-bound for months
- The catalyst: earnings beat, FDA approval, contract win, acquisition news, or government contract
- The result: a gap up of 8–15%+ on 2–5× average daily volume
- The trade: enter on the opening range breakout, hold for a multi-week or multi-month move

---

## Why Episodic Pivots Work

1. **Forced Institutional Buying:** When a company announces dramatically better-than-expected results, fund managers are *required* to add it to their portfolios. They can't do this in one day — they buy for weeks afterward.

2. **Revaluation Event:** The market suddenly reprices the stock at a higher P/E multiple, reflecting the new growth trajectory. This is not just a short-term spike — it's a fundamental repricing.

3. **Short Squeeze Component:** Often, stocks that gap up 10%+ on earnings have high short interest. As short sellers scramble to cover, this adds buying pressure.

4. **Media and Retail FOMO:** Stocks that post huge earnings gaps attract retail attention, adding buying fuel.

## Ideal Episodic Pivot Setup Criteria

### Pre-Catalyst Conditions
- Stock has been in a **multi-week or multi-month base** before the event (range-bound, declining volume)
- Ideally, the stock has been **below or at the 50-day moving average** (underowned by institutions)
- High short interest is a bonus (potential short squeeze)
- Stock has liquidity: > 300,000 shares average daily volume

### The Catalyst Event
- Earnings beat: EPS surprise of 20%+ above estimates, or revenue above estimates by 10%+
- Major regulatory approval (FDA, FCC, etc.)
- Government contract announcement
- Acquisition news or major partnership
- Management change (for turnaround stories)

### The Gap Day Requirements
- **Gap size:** 8%+ above prior close (10–20%+ is ideal)
- **Opening volume:** Should reach 100%+ of the stock's *full average daily volume* within the first 15–20 minutes
- **Price action:** Stock should trade above the opening price for most of the session
- **Closing strength:** Ideally closes in the top 50% of the day's range
- The gap should be the **largest one-day move** in the last 6–12 months

### Rejection Signals (Do NOT Trade)
- Stock gaps up but immediately reverses and trades below the open
- Opening volume is normal or below average
- The gap fills on the same day (stock returns to previous close)
- Broad market is in a severe downtrend on the same day
- The catalyst was already known/priced in (no surprise element)

## Entry Methods

### Method 1: Intraday Opening Range Breakout (Aggressive)
**Timing:** First 5–15 minutes after market open

1. Let the stock trade for the first 5 minutes after open
2. Mark the **Opening Range High (ORH)** and **Opening Range Low (ORL)** from the first 1-minute or 5-minute candle
3. Enter a buy-stop order at 5 cents above the ORH
4. Place stop loss at the ORL (or 0.5% below)
5. Volume must be expanding when the ORH is broken

**Pros:** Captures the full move from the open
**Cons:** More risk, requires active monitoring, can have false breaks

### Method 2: Opening Range High Break (Standard)
**Timing:** First 30 minutes

1. Watch the first 30 minutes of trading
2. Note the 30-minute high
3. Buy on a break above the 30-minute high if volume is still surging
4. Stop below the 30-minute low

### Method 3: Next-Day Continuation
**Timing:** 1–5 days after the gap day

1. Let the gap day settle
2. Watch for a 1–5 day mini-consolidation near the high of the gap day
3. Buy on the break above the mini-base high
4. Volume should pick back up on the breakout
5. Stop below the mini-base low or the gap day low

**Pros:** Safer entry, more confirmation
**Cons:** Misses the initial surge, requires patience

## Position Sizing for EP Trades

The EP is typically a higher-conviction trade and deserves appropriate sizing, but risk rules still apply:

```
Position Size = Dollar Risk / (Entry – Stop)
Dollar Risk = Account × 0.5% to 1%
Max position size = 20% of account (overnight)
Max intraday position = 50% of account (only with active monitoring)

Example:
Account = $100,000
Risk % = 0.5%
Dollar Risk = $500
Entry = $50.00
Stop = $48.00 (gap day low)
Risk per share = $2.00
Shares = $500 / $2 = 250 shares
Position value = 250 × $50 = $12,500 (12.5% of account — OK)
```

## Exit Rules for Episodic Pivots

### Short-Term Exit (If Trade Fails)
- If price reverses below the opening range low → **exit immediately, no exceptions**
- If price closes back below the gap day low → exit full position
- If volume on down days exceeds volume on up days → distribution signal, reduce or exit

### Partial Profit Taking
- First profit target: **2R** (2× your initial risk) — sell 1/3 to 1/2
- After first sale: move stop to breakeven
- Second profit target: **3R** — sell another 1/4

### Trailing the Remainder
- Use the **10-day moving average** as a trailing stop
- Sell remaining position on a **close below the 10-day MA**
- For stronger setups, trail with the **20-day MA** to capture larger moves

### Extended Hold Rules
- If the stock forms a new base after the EP and breaks out again → this is a **continuation breakout**
- Consider adding back to the position at the continuation breakout
- Trail with higher time-frame (20-day or 50-day MA)

## Episodic Pivot vs. Standard Breakout

| Feature | Episodic Pivot | Standard Breakout |
|---------|---------------|-------------------|
| Prior base required? | No (can gap from flat range) | Yes (30%+ prior move) |
| Catalyst required? | Yes — essential | Preferred but not required |
| Volume on entry day | Massive (5–10× normal) | 1.5–2× normal |
| Time to setup | Immediate (day of event) | Weeks of base building |
| Risk on entry | Can be higher (gap up) | Tight stop below base |
| Expected move | Large and fast | Large and sustained |
| Frequency | Rare (tied to news events) | Common (screen any time) |

## Famous Episodic Pivot Examples

### 1. Netflix (NFLX) — Multiple Occasions
- NFLX has had several EPs: Q3 2013, Q2 2018, Q4 2022
- Each was driven by subscriber beats or revenue revisions
- Each led to 30–80%+ moves in weeks to months

### 2. Nvidia (NVDA) — May 2023 (AI Earnings EP)
- NVDA reported May 2023 earnings with a massive revenue guide raise due to AI demand
- Gap: +25% in one day
- Volume: 4–5× normal
- Move after EP: NVDA tripled over the next 12 months

### 3. Meta (META) — Q2 2023
- META reported strong earnings after a year of losses
- Gap: +20% in one day
- Volume: massive institutional buying
- Move: META doubled from the EP high over the following 12 months

### 4. Palantir (PLTR) — November 2024
- PLTR reported strong AI-driven earnings
- Gap: 30%+ on huge volume
- Formed a 3-week mini-base and broke out again
- Total move from EP: 100%+ in 3 months

## Screening for Episodic Pivots

To scan for EPs, look for:
1. Today's gap vs. prior close: ≥ 8%
2. Opening volume vs. 20-day average: ≥ 200% by 10 AM
3. Stock is NOT reversing — holding above the opening price
4. Catalyst confirmed: earnings/news visible in financial data
5. Stock is in a recognizable sector with a meaningful theme

- Finviz: Filter for `Price gap > 8%`, `Volume > 200% of average`, `Today's performance > 8%`
